Social media shouldn't be a popularity contest.

Likes, followers and a post that "did well". None of it is a patient. This is a letter about the difference.

Dear Clinic Builder,

Tonight, somewhere near your clinic, a woman is scrolling Instagram on the sofa. She has been thinking about her eyelids for two years. She has never typed "blepharoplasty" into Google. She is not ready to search. But she is ready to stop scrolling, if the right surgeon is in front of her at the right moment.

That is what Facebook and Instagram are for.

Most clinics use them for something else.

They post a before-and-after, boost it for $50, and get a few hundred likes, mostly from other clinics and people who live interstate. Or they hire an agency that sends a report full of reach, impressions and engagement rate, and goes quiet when asked how many consultations it booked. Meta will happily take your money for attention. Turning that attention into patients is a different job.

It takes ads that stop the thumb in the first second, a form that asks the right questions, and somebody calling the lead back while she still remembers filling it in. Miss any one of those and the cheapest lead in the world is worthless.

So do not believe a word we say. Further down this page are the Ads Manager screenshots, the cost of each lead, and more than a hundred of the actual video ads we made for clinics. Watch them, then decide.

Our 6-step Facebook and Instagram ads process.

It is a sequence, not a menu. We test small before we spend big, because on social the ad you expect to win usually doesn't.

01

Look before you spend.

The first fortnight is spent learning what you have, and what your competitors are already running.

  • 1
    Asset analysisYour past and current Facebook and Instagram campaigns, their settings, results and the ads themselves, plus the photos, videos and reviews we have to work with. That gives us a baseline to beat.
  • 2
    Competitor reviewEvery ad your direct competitors are running in the Meta Ad Library, and the clinics that solve the same problem differently. What they say, what they offer, and the gap you can own.
02

Agree what winning means, then test it.

A small budget proves which procedure, audience and ad will work before the large one is spent.

  • 3
    Success definitionTogether we choose the procedures to advertise, the patients to reach, and the numbers that matter: cost per lead, consultations booked, procedures done. The budget is set to hit them, and every ad is checked against Meta's health policies, AHPRA's advertising guidelines and the TGA's rules first.
  • 4
    Campaign testA small launch with several ads, hooks and forms running side by side, so the market tells us which message and which offer brings in leads fastest.
03

Scale what works, then keep improving.

Month one gives you leads and data. By month three the winning ads have been rebuilt, retargeted and scaled.

  • 5
    Campaign expansionBudget moves to the procedures, audiences and ads that won the test, step by step, with your front desk telling us which leads turned into consultations.
  • 6
    Continuous improvementNew creative before the old one tires, remarketing to everyone who engaged, and a written monthly report. If a number cannot be traced to a patient, it does not make the report.

Sensible questions, straight answers.

The swagger stops here. These are the questions Australian clinic owners ask us about Facebook and Instagram advertising on strategy sessions, answered the way we answer them.

Do Facebook and Instagram ads actually work for doctors and clinics?
For the right procedures, yes. They work best where patients think for weeks or months before booking and like to see the result and the surgeon first: facelifts, blepharoplasty, breast and body surgery, hair transplants, laser eye surgery, aesthetics, men's health. They work less well for urgent, one-off needs, where Google Ads usually wins. The results section above shows the leads and cost per lead for each specialty.
How much does a lead from Facebook or Instagram cost?
It depends on the procedure, the city and the offer. In the Australian campaigns on this page, leads cost from about $6 for Morpheus8 and $8 for anti-wrinkle treatment to $11–14 for hair transplants and breast augmentation. What matters more is how many leads book a consultation, which is why we track them past the form.
How much should a clinic spend on Meta ads?
Most of the campaigns on this page run on $10 to $70 a day each, one campaign per procedure. On the strategy session we look at your procedures, your area and your competitors' ads, and give you a budget range with an expected cost per lead before you commit to anything. Your ad spend is paid directly to Meta on your own card, separate from our fee.
Instant forms or a landing page?
Usually both, tested against each other. Instant forms open inside Facebook and Instagram, so more people fill them in and leads are cheaper. A landing page asks a little more effort and tends to bring fewer, warmer enquiries. We add qualifying questions to the form so your team knows who to call first, and keep whichever produces more consultations, not more leads.
Are social media leads lower quality than Google leads?
They are earlier in the decision, not worse. Someone who fills in a form between videos needs a call back the same day and a good reason to book. That is why every lead goes into your CRM with an instant SMS and email, and why we report consultations booked, not just leads.
What can a medical ad say on Facebook and Instagram?
Less than most clinics expect. Meta does not allow ads that imply they know someone's health condition ("Struggling with hair loss?"), restricts before-and-after images, and stopped letting advertisers target health interests in 2022. In Australia, the TGA does not allow prescription-only medicines such as anti-wrinkle injections to be advertised to the public, and AHPRA and the Medical Board’s guidelines restrict testimonials, before-and-after images and cosmetic procedure ads aimed at under-18s. We check every ad against Meta's policies, AHPRA’s advertising guidelines and the TGA’s rules before it runs.
Do you make the ads, or do we have to supply them?
We make them. The videos in the Our work section were scripted, edited and run by our team, from surgeon explainers to patient stories and clinic tours. If you can give us a short session in front of a camera, or your own footage, we turn it into a set of ads to test. Photos and reviews you already have are a good place to start.
Do Facebook ads work for dentists?
Yes, for treatments patients plan for rather than emergencies: implants, Invisalign and aligners, veneers, whitening and smile makeovers. The same rules apply as for any clinic: one treatment per campaign, a clear price or offer, and a fast call back.
Who owns the ad account and the leads?
You do. We work inside your own Meta Business account, billed to your card, so the ads, the audiences, the pixel data and every lead stay with your clinic whatever happens.
Should we run social ads or Google Ads?
Ideally both, for different jobs. Google Ads catches the patients already searching for your procedure. Facebook and Instagram reach the far larger group who haven't started searching yet, and remind the searchers who visited and left. Most of our clinics start with whichever is closer to a booked patient for their procedure, then add the other.
